### Repeaters ([Physical Layer](1.%20Physical%20Layer.md))

**1. 2 Port Device**

- A repeater is a network device that operates at the physical layer (Layer 1) of the OSI model.
- It consists of two ports: one for receiving data from one network segment and another for sending it to another segment.
- The primary purpose of a repeater is to extend the range of a network by regenerating signals that are weak or distorted as they travel over a medium.

**2. Forwarding**

- Repeaters forward data packets without any modification or interpretation.
- They simply amplify the incoming signal and transmit it out through the other port.
- As a result, the repeater does not make decisions about traffic routing; it acts purely as a signal booster, enhancing the quality and distance of the signal transmission.

**3. No Filtering**

- Unlike switches or routers, repeaters do not filter data packets or manage traffic.
- They do not inspect or analyze the data being transmitted; they operate on the principle of pure signal regeneration.
- This lack of filtering means that all data packets are sent to the next segment without any consideration of their source or destination addresses.

**4. Collision Domain - 'n'**

- A repeater creates a single collision domain that encompasses all connected segments.
- This means that if two devices connected to the repeater transmit data simultaneously, a collision can occur, resulting in data loss.
- The number of devices (n) that can be connected through a repeater will influence the network's performance, as more devices lead to a higher likelihood of collisions.
- As a result, while repeaters can extend network distances, they can also introduce latency and performance degradation if the collision domain becomes too crowded.

### Repeaters vs Amplifiers

- **Repeaters** regenerate and forward digital signals to extend network range without filtering or modifying data, operating purely at the physical layer.
- In contrast, **amplifiers** boost the strength of analog signals to enhance transmission quality but do not regenerate the original signal, which can lead to distortion over long distances.

### Summary

In summary, repeaters are simple yet essential devices in network infrastructure, mainly used for extending the distance of communication within a local area network (LAN). However, due to their nature of forwarding all signals without filtering and the creation of a single collision domain, they are more suited for smaller network segments rather than complex, high-traffic environments.
