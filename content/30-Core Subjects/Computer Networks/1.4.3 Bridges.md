### Bridges ([Physical](1.%20Physical%20Layer.md) and [Data Link Layer](2.%20Data%20Link%20Layer.md))

**1. Connect Two Different LANs**

- Bridges are network devices that connect two or more local area networks (LANs), allowing them to function as a single cohesive network.
- By connecting different LAN segments, bridges help extend the network, improve performance, and facilitate communication between devices on separate networks.

**2. Forwarding**

- Bridges forward data frames based on the destination MAC (Media Access Control) addresses.
- Unlike hubs, which broadcast to all ports, bridges intelligently send frames only to the segment containing the destination device, reducing unnecessary traffic and improving network efficiency.

**3. Filtering**

- Bridges have the capability to filter traffic by examining the MAC addresses of incoming frames.
- If the destination address of a frame matches a device on the same segment, the bridge does not forward the frame to other segments, effectively reducing collisions and network load.

**4. Collision Domain**

- Each segment connected by a bridge constitutes a separate collision domain.
- This means that devices within the same segment can still collide with each other, but traffic between different segments is managed independently, thus minimizing collisions and enhancing overall network performance.

**5. Bridge Data Unit Control**

- Bridges utilize Bridge Data Units (BDUs) to manage and control the flow of data across the connected LANs.
- BDUs help in making forwarding decisions based on the MAC address table maintained by the bridge, which maps MAC addresses to the corresponding ports.

**6. Bridge Uses Store and Forward Strategy**

- Bridges typically employ a store-and-forward strategy, where they receive entire data frames before forwarding them.
- This allows the bridge to check the frame for errors (using checksums) and make intelligent forwarding decisions based on the frame's destination MAC address, enhancing data integrity and efficiency.

**7. Bridge Types: Static, Dynamic, or Transparent**

- **Static Bridges:** These bridges have a predefined MAC address table and do not learn new addresses dynamically. They are less flexible but can be simpler to manage in small networks.
- **Dynamic Bridges:** These bridges can learn and update their MAC address tables dynamically as devices connect and disconnect, making them more adaptable to changes in the network.
- **Transparent Bridges:** These bridges forward frames based on their MAC addresses without modifying the original data frame, ensuring seamless integration into existing networks.

### Summary

In summary, bridges are essential networking devices that connect different LANs, forwarding and filtering data based on MAC addresses to reduce traffic and collisions. By utilizing a store-and-forward strategy and maintaining a dynamic MAC address table, bridges improve the efficiency and performance of network communications across multiple segments.
