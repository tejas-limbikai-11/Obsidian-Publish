### Switches (Layer-2 ([Data Link Layer](2.%20Data%20Link%20Layer.md)) Device)

**1. Multiport Bridge**

- A switch is essentially a multiport [bridge](1.4.3%20Bridges.md) that connects multiple devices within a local area network (LAN).
- It operates at the data link layer (Layer 2) of the OSI model, forwarding data frames based on their MAC addresses, and allowing for efficient communication between connected devices.

**2. Full Duplex Links**

- Switches support full-duplex communication, enabling simultaneous data transmission and reception on the same link.
- This capability significantly increases network performance, as devices can send and receive data at the same time without collisions, improving overall throughput.

**3. Traffic is Minimal**

- Switches intelligently forward data only to the intended recipient device rather than broadcasting to all ports.
- This targeted forwarding minimizes unnecessary traffic on the network, reducing congestion and improving bandwidth utilization, which enhances the performance of network applications.

**4. Collision Domain is Zero**

- Each port on a switch represents its own collision domain, effectively eliminating collisions between devices connected to different ports.
- This design allows multiple devices to communicate simultaneously without interference, resulting in a more efficient network environment compared to traditional hubs or repeaters.

### Summary

In summary, switches are advanced networking devices that operate as multiport bridges, utilizing full-duplex links to enable efficient communication between connected devices. By minimizing traffic through intelligent forwarding and eliminating collisions with separate collision domains for each port, switches enhance network performance and reliability in local area networks.
