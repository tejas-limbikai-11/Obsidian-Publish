## Types of Networks: PAN, LAN, CAN, MAN, WAN

This chart compares different types of networks based on various characteristics such as technology, range, transmission speed, and more.

![3. Types of Networks - PAN, LAN, CAN, MAN, WAN](00-Attachments/3. Types of Networks - PAN, LAN, CAN, MAN, WAN.png)

---

### 1. **PAN (Personal Area Network)**

- **Full Form**: Personal Area Network
- **Technology**: Bluetooth, IrDA (Infrared Data Association), Zigbee
- **Range**: 1-100 meters
- **Transmission Speed**: Very High
- **Area**: Typically used within a single room or for short-range communication between devices (e.g., between a phone and a wireless headset).
- **Ownership**: Private (usually owned by an individual or a specific device owner).
- **Maintenance**: Very Easy (low complexity, minimal infrastructure).
- **Error Rate & Cost**: Very Low (due to short range and limited interference).

---

### 2. **LAN (Local Area Network)**

- **Full Form**: Local Area Network
- **Technology**: Ethernet and Wi-Fi
- **Range**: Up to 2 kilometers
- **Transmission Speed**: Very High
- **Area**: Used within offices, buildings, or homes to connect computers and devices to share resources like printers and internet access.
- **Ownership**: Private (owned by organizations or individuals).
- **Maintenance**: Easy (LANs are typically managed by local administrators).
- **Error Rate & Cost**: Low (due to relatively small coverage areas and controlled environments).

---

### 3. **CAN (Campus Area Network)**

- **Full Form**: Campus Area Network
- **Technology**: Ethernet
- **Range**: 1-5 kilometers
- **Transmission Speed**: High
- **Area**: Used within universities, corporate campuses, or large organizations to connect multiple LANs.
- **Ownership**: Private (typically owned by the organization, university, or corporation).
- **Maintenance**: Moderate (larger network size than LAN requires some level of IT management).
- **Error Rate & Cost**: Moderate (larger area introduces more chances for errors and higher costs).

---

### 4. **MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)**

- **Full Form**: Metropolitan Area Network
- **Technology**: FDDI (Fiber Distributed Data Interface), CDDI (Copper Distributed Data Interface), ATM (Asynchronous Transfer Mode)
- **Range**: 5-50 kilometers
- **Transmission Speed**: Average
- **Area**: Covers cities or large metropolitan areas. Used by ISPs or organizations to connect multiple LANs within a city.
- **Ownership**: Private or Public (may be owned by service providers or public authorities).
- **Maintenance**: Difficult (larger network spanning cities, more complex management).
- **Error Rate & Cost**: High (due to longer distances and more potential interference).

---

### 5. **WAN (Wide Area Network)**

- **Full Form**: Wide Area Network
- **Technology**: Leased lines, Dial-Up
- **Range**: Above 50 kilometers (can span countries or even continents)
- **Transmission Speed**: Low
- **Area**: Used to connect devices across countries or continents, often through satellite, leased lines, or undersea cables. The Internet is the largest example of a WAN.
- **Ownership**: Private or Public (large-scale ownership, often by telecommunications companies or governments).
- **Maintenance**: Very Difficult (complex systems with vast coverage, multiple stakeholders involved).
- **Error Rate & Cost**: Very High (long distances and various technologies increase the error rates and costs).

---

### Summary

- **PAN** is for personal devices over a short range, with high speed and low maintenance.
- **LAN** is commonly used in homes and offices, covering a small area with very high transmission speeds.
- **CAN** connects LANs over larger areas like campuses, offering high-speed connections with moderate complexity.
- **MAN** serves cities, with average speeds but higher complexity and costs due to the wide area.
- **WAN** connects geographically distant networks, but with lower transmission speeds and very high costs and error rates due to the scale of the network.

Each type of network serves different purposes depending on the area of coverage, speed requirements, and ownership, balancing complexity and maintenance effort based on its intended scope.
