## Types of Network Topologies

Network topology refers to the **arrangement** of various network elements, such as cables and devices, and how they are interconnected. Here's a detailed overview of four common types of topologies: **Mesh**, **Hub**, **Bus**, and **Ring**, based on their characteristics like cables, ports, reliability, cost, and security.

---

### 1. **Mesh Topology**

![5. Topologies in Computer Networks - Mesh Topology](00-Attachments/5. Topologies in Computer Networks - Mesh Topology.png)

- **Number of Cables**: n \* (n - 1) / 2  (where ( n ) is the number of devices)
  - In a mesh topology, every device is connected to every other device, requiring a large number of cables.

- **Number of Ports**: n \* (n-1)
  - Each device needs a port for every other device it connects to.

- **Reliability**: **High**
  - Since devices are interconnected, even if one connection fails, there are alternate paths for data transmission.

- **Cost**: **High**
  - The cost is high due to the large number of cables and ports required for full connectivity.

- **Security**: **High**
  - Data travels through dedicated links between devices, making it harder for unauthorized users to intercept the communication.

- **Connection Type**: Point-to-point
  - Each connection is directly between two devices.

**Use Case**: Mesh topology is typically used in critical systems where high reliability and redundancy are essential, such as in data centers or military networks.

---

### 2. **Hub Topology** (Star Topology)

![5. Topologies in Computer Networks - Hub Topology](00-Attachments/5. Topologies in Computer Networks - Hub Topology.png)

- **Number of Cables**: n
  - Each device connects to a central hub using a single cable, with the hub managing traffic.

- **Number of Ports**: n
  - The hub has one port for each device connected to it.

- **Reliability**: **Low**
  - If the hub fails, the entire network goes down. However, if a single cable fails, only the connected device is affected.

- **Cost**: **Low**
  - With fewer cables and a centralized hub, this topology is less expensive compared to a mesh topology.

- **Security**: **Low**
  - Since all traffic passes through the hub, it is easier for a malicious actor to intercept or tamper with data.

- **Connection Type**: Hub-based
  - All devices communicate via the hub.

**Use Case**: Hub topology is common in home or small office networks due to its simplicity and low cost.

---

### 3. **Bus Topology**

![5. Topologies in Computer Networks - Bus Topology](00-Attachments/5. Topologies in Computer Networks - Bus Topology.png)

- **Number of Cables**: n + 1
  - A single backbone cable (bus) is used to connect all devices, and each device is connected to the bus via a smaller cable.

- **Number of Ports**: n
  - Each device needs one port to connect to the backbone cable.

- **Reliability**: **Low**
  - If the backbone cable fails, the entire network fails. Additionally, collisions can occur, leading to lower reliability.

- **Cost**: **Low**
  - The use of a single backbone cable makes this topology relatively inexpensive to set up.

- **Security**: **Low**
  - All devices share the same communication medium, making it easy for an unauthorized device to listen to traffic.

- **Connection Type**: Shared medium
  - All devices share the same communication channel.

**Use Case**: Bus topology is used in small networks or temporary setups where cost is a major factor and traffic load is low.

---

### 4. **Ring Topology**

![5. Topologies in Computer Networks - Ring Topology](00-Attachments/5. Topologies in Computer Networks - Ring Topology.png)

- **Number of Cables**: n + 1
  - Each device is connected to two other devices, forming a circular loop. Data travels in one direction around the ring.

- **Number of Ports**: n
  - Each device needs one port to connect to its immediate neighbors in the ring.

- **Reliability**: **Low**
  - If one cable or device fails, the entire network can be disrupted unless it is a dual-ring setup (which adds redundancy).

- **Cost**: **Low**
  - It requires fewer cables and ports compared to mesh topology, keeping costs low.

- **Security**: **Low**
  - Since data is shared among all devices in the ring, there is a greater risk of data being intercepted.

- **Connection Type**: Point-to-point in a circular fashion
  - Devices are connected in a closed loop.

**Use Case**: Ring topology is used in small networks where redundancy is not critical, such as within small departments or labs.

---

### Summary of Characteristics

| **Topology** | **No. of Cables** | **No. of Ports** | **Reliability** | **Cost** | **Security** |
| :----------: | :---------------: | :--------------: | :-------------: | :------: | :----------: |
|   **Mesh**   |     n(n-1)/2      |      n(n-1)      |      High       |   High   |     High     |
|   **Hub**    |         n         |        n         |       Low       |   Low    |     Low      |
|   **Bus**    |       n + 1       |        n         |       Low       |   Low    |     Low      |
|   **Ring**   |       n + 1       |        n         |       Low       |   Low    |     Low      |

Each topology has its pros and cons depending on factors like reliability, cost, and security. The choice of topology depends on the specific requirements and scale of the network being designed.
