### Devices and Their Effects on Collision and Broadcast Domains

1. [Repeater](1.4.1%20Repeaters.md)
   - **Collision Domain**: No Change\
     A repeater operates at the physical layer (Layer 1) of the OSI model and merely amplifies and regenerates signals. It does not segment the network; therefore, the collision domain remains unchanged.
   - **Broadcast Domain**: No Change\
     Since a repeater does not filter or separate traffic, the broadcast domain is also unchanged. All broadcasts are forwarded to all connected devices.

2. [Hub](1.4.2%20Hubs.md)
   - **Collision Domain**: No Change\
     Hubs also operate at the physical layer and act as multiport repeaters. They transmit data to all connected devices, keeping a single collision domain.
   - **Broadcast Domain**: No Change\
     Similar to repeaters, hubs do not filter broadcast traffic, resulting in no change to the broadcast domain.

3. [Bridge](1.4.3%20Bridges.md)
   - **Collision Domain**: Reduce\
     Bridges operate at the data link layer (Layer 2) and can segment a network into multiple collision domains. Each port on a bridge creates a separate collision domain, reducing the likelihood of collisions by limiting the traffic on each segment.
   - **Broadcast Domain**: No Change\
     Bridges forward all broadcast packets to all ports, so the broadcast domain remains unchanged.

4. [Switch](1.4.4%20Switches.md)
   - **Collision Domain**: Reduce\
     Switches also function at the data link layer and provide dedicated collision domains for each port. This means that devices connected to different switch ports can transmit simultaneously without causing collisions, thus significantly reducing the number of collisions on the network.
   - **Broadcast Domain**: No Change\
     Like bridges, switches forward broadcast packets to all ports. As a result, the broadcast domain remains the same, encompassing all devices connected to the switch.

5. [Router](1.4.5%20Routers.md)
   - **Collision Domain**: Reduce\
     Routers operate at the network layer (Layer 3) and can segment collision domains. Each interface on a router creates a separate collision domain, thus reducing the overall number of collisions on the network.
   - **Broadcast Domain**: Reduce\
     Routers also segment broadcast domains. They do not forward broadcast packets from one interface to another, which helps to isolate and reduce broadcast traffic within a network.

### Summary

- **Collision Domains**: Devices like bridges, switches, and routers help to reduce collision domains by segmenting the network and allowing for simultaneous transmissions without interference. Repeaters and hubs do not change collision domains.
- **Broadcast Domains**: Routers reduce broadcast domains by not forwarding broadcast packets across different segments, while repeaters, hubs, and switches maintain the same broadcast domain since they forward broadcast messages to all connected devices.
