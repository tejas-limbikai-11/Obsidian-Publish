### Circuit Switching in Computer Networks

Circuit switching is a method of communication where a **dedicated communication path** is established between two devices for the duration of the entire transmission. This is typically used in traditional telephony systems but is less common in modern data networks. Below is a detailed explanation based on the provided points:

1. **[Physical Layer](1.%20Physical%20Layer.md)**
   Circuit switching occurs at the physical layer of the OSI model, which deals with the actual physical connection between devices. It ensures a dedicated path for data transmission.

2. **Dedicated Path**\
   In circuit switching, a dedicated path is established between the sender and receiver. This path remains reserved for the entire duration of the session, ensuring that the data sent by the sender is delivered to the receiver without interruption.

3. **Contiguous Flow**\
   Data flows in a **contiguous stream** along the reserved path once the circuit is established. There is no need to wait for data packets to be reassembled, as data is transmitted as a continuous flow of bits.

4. **No Headers**\
   Since circuit switching establishes a dedicated path beforehand, there is no need to include headers with each data transmission to indicate the source, destination, or other routing information. The entire path is predetermined and fixed.

5. **In Order Transmission**\
   In circuit-switched networks, data is guaranteed to arrive in the same order it was sent because the path is constant and reserved. This eliminates the need for complex reordering mechanisms.

6. **Efficiency – Less**\
   Circuit switching is considered inefficient for data transmission, especially in modern networks, because the dedicated path remains reserved even when no data is being sent. This leads to wasted bandwidth during idle times, reducing overall network efficiency.

7. **Delay – Less**\
   Although the setup time may introduce some initial delay, once the circuit is established, data transmission experiences minimal delay due to the reserved, direct path. There is no need for packet routing or reassembly, making it faster than packet-switching methods in terms of transmission delay.

### Timing Considerations in Circuit Switching

1. **Total Time**\
   The total time to send data in a circuit-switched network can be calculated as the sum of several factors:  \$\$
   \text{Total Time} = \text{Setup Time} + \text{Transmission Time} + \\
   \text{Propagation Delay} + \text{Tear Down Time}

$$
   - **Setup Time**: The time required to establish the dedicated path between the sender and receiver.
   - **Transmission Time**: The time required to transmit the entire message once the circuit is established.
   - **Propagation Delay**: The time it takes for the signal to travel through the medium from the sender to the receiver.
   - **Tear Down Time**: The time required to release the circuit after the transmission is complete.

2. **Transmission Time**  
   This is the time required to transmit the entire message and is calculated as:
$$

\text{Transmission Time} = \frac{\text{Message Size}}{\text{Bandwidth}}

$$
Where:
- **Message Size** is the total amount of data being sent.
- **Bandwidth** is the data transfer rate of the communication link.

3. **Propagation Delay**  
The time it takes for the signal to propagate through the medium is given by:
$$

\text{Propagation Delay} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed of Signal}}

$$
Where:
- **Distance** is the physical distance between the sender and receiver.
- **Speed of Signal** is the speed at which the signal travels, usually close to the speed of light in the transmission medium (e.g., fiber optics, copper wire).

### Summary
Circuit switching ensures **low delay and in-order delivery** of data through a **dedicated path** established at the physical layer, but its **efficiency is low** due to the constant reservation of resources. Despite its initial setup time, once the connection is established, it allows for fast, reliable data transmission with minimal delays.
